# v0.3.2 Fabric, Surfing, And Camera Review

**Status:** Deferred design review. Do not start during v0.3.1 correction, RC,
build, or Deck work.

**Sequence gate:** Begin only after the v0.3.1 Inhibitor cap/reset, exfil/timer,
and entity-size/sprite readability changes have been built and reviewed on the
physical Deck. Those changes define the immutable visual baseline.

## Product Question

At crowded density the fabric can become visually chaotic even when simulation
truth is correct. Review how the scene teaches surfable flow, force ownership,
hazards, landmarks, and route opportunity without calming it into a generic
background. Freeze authoritative movement, gravity, 15 Hz fidelity, map scale,
entity forces, Noise radii, collision, and surfing physics during this review.

Camera work must separate physical Deck readability from dramatic framing.
Desktop framing is broadly acceptable; Deck may need a closer platform or
accessibility baseline, presentation-scale compensation, or both. Entity count
must never directly drive zoom-out.

## Review Method

1. Capture four settled scenes at 1280x800: empty/static, ordinary traversal,
   multi-force/objective, and capped late match.
2. Inventory each fabric, wake, force, hazard, Noise, entity, label, camera, and
   degradation contributor: owner, cost, layer/blend, lifetime, and gameplay
   meaning.
3. Compare a conservative hierarchy/aggregation direction with at least one
   bolder art direction in motion.
4. Compare three camera models in the same scenes:
   - fixed closer Deck baseline;
   - bounded phase-authored framing, closer early and modestly wider later;
   - restrained context-aware framing with hysteresis for justified states such
     as high-speed travel, slingshot, exfil approach, or a large heard threat.
5. Measure physical Deck object and text sizes against desktop, then define
   minimum player, enemy, wreck, well, exfil, label, and fabric-feature sizes at
   every allowed zoom.

The review must decide the visual grammar for surfable versus dangerous flow,
how many signals remain individually legible, what aggregates into one field
response, what persists or decays with distance/density, and which visual
quality reduces first under Deck load. Camera comparison must cover surf-line
legibility, horizon awareness, perceived speed, slingshot timing, crowding,
motion sickness, hard min/max zoom, transition duration, deadbands, and states
that prohibit a zoom change.

## Decision Gate

Return motion comparisons and explicit options to Greg before broad
implementation. Greg chooses the camera model, Deck baseline, and fabric
hierarchy. Tests protect lifecycle and performance only; human play owns visual
taste. No implementation, capture, Orrery request, ImageGen, or test work is
authorized by this entry.
