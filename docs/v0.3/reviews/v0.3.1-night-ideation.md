# v0.3.1 Night Ideation — Five Areas, Options, Pros/Cons

> Overnight ideation pass 2026-07-14→15 (Orrery, per Greg's night-shift
> brief). High concept for future in-build direction — nothing here is
> implementation. Areas 1–2 feed the hand-authored endgame map
> (`v0.3.1-endgame-map-undertow.md`).

## Area 1 — The Anchor Question (S9a rethink)

What anchors a dying galaxy, if not a guaranteed Gargantua?

### Option 1A — Guaranteed Gargantua (status quo S9a)
Every map carries THE supermassive.
**Pros:** title made literal; orientation constant; endgame always has a
stage; art budget concentrates on one showpiece.
**Cons:** Greg's doubt is right — every galaxy identical at the center
homogenizes maps; fights the encounter-table variety thesis; weakens the
dying-universe theme (collapse should be everywhere, not one drain).

### Option 1B — The anchor is a draw
The XL dominant slot draws per map: supermassive, binary supermassive,
quasar core, or the Roil itself as the gravitational center.
**Pros:** "which universe is this" starts at the anchor; tables stay
consistent per tier; orientation generalizes (the sea leans toward the
heaviest mass, whatever it is).
**Cons:** endgame staging must generalize across anchor types; art
budget spreads; the title loses its literal referent.

### Option 1C — The Singularity is off-map
The supermassive is the destination *beyond* every map. Maps are regions
of its watershed, falling toward it. Its influence is the global
anisotropy — everything drifts one direction, faintly, always — but it
is never on screen.
**Pros:** strongest thematic fit — you are always inside its pull and
never at it; solves toroid orientation globally and permanently; every
map's visible cast is fully free; the title becomes mythic instead of
literal ("the last singularity" is where all of this is going).
**Cons:** loses the Interstellar showpiece; the endgame needs a local
expression of "the fall accelerates"; harder to teach in one screenshot.

### Option 1D — Watershed + local drains (hybrid, RECOMMENDED)
1C's off-map Singularity provides the permanent anisotropy ("downhill"
exists on every map); each map's XL dominant slot (1B) draws a **local
drain** — the nearest mouth of the watershed: a supermassive, a binary,
a quasar core. Collapse feeds the local drain; the local drain feeds the
thing you never see.
**Pros:** permanent orientation + per-map anchor variety + endgame stage
guaranteed (the local drain) + the theme at full strength — every
universe dies differently, all of it drains to the same place.
**Cons:** two-layer gravity fiction to teach (mitigated: players only
feel one — "everything drifts that way; the big thing over there is
why, locally").

**Orrery recommendation: 1D**, with 1C as the fallback if art budget
forces a single showpiece anyway. Kills the sameness problem, keeps the
stage, and makes the title bigger than any one map.

## Area 2 — The Map Definition Pass (exfil/spawn geography)

What IS a map, such that spawn and exfil sites have principled homes?

### Option 2A — Ring grammar
Anchor-centric bands: danger core → encounter belt → slack rim. Spawns
and exfil pool on the rim; risk gradient radial.
**Pros:** instantly legible risk geography; classic extraction flow
(edge in, center loot, edge out); trivial WFC priors.
**Cons:** on a toroid the "rim" is arbitrary — it only exists relative
to the anchor, so all maps share one polar shape; symmetric and samey;
fights the set-pieces-move thesis.

### Option 2B — Watershed grammar (current-defined)
The map is defined by its flow field. Spawns sit in slack water
upstream; loot concentrates at confluences and eddies (where drifting
material collects — which is *why* wrecks are there, diegetically);
danger accumulates downstream toward the drain; exfils sit cross-stream
(extraction is never a drift — you commit across the current).
**Pros:** the sea is the geography — movement, navigation, risk, and
loot placement all read from one field; pairs perfectly with 1D (the
drain is downstream by definition); every draw reshapes the watershed so
geography varies with the cast; deeply on-theme (salvage collects where
the current says).
**Cons:** depends on the authored sea being implemented and legible
(Wave 1 + S8); risk gradient is subtler than rings; generation must
solve flow before placement (ordering constraint).

### Option 2C — Constellation grammar
Set pieces are graph nodes with authored edge lengths (travel-time
budgets); corridors are the edges, breadcrumbed; spawns/exfils occupy
leaf positions on the graph periphery.
**Pros:** direct authorial control of pacing per route; Lynchian
nodes-and-paths legibility; equally good for hand-authoring and
generation; travel-time budgeting is native to it.
**Cons:** can read as a board-game graph if edges are too clean; needs
the flow field layered on top anyway or movement feels off-theme.

### Recommendation: 2B as identity, 2C as skeleton
Author maps as constellations (nodes, edge budgets — the craft tool),
then let the watershed give the constellation its physics and its
diegesis: edges follow or fight currents, confluences justify loot,
slack water justifies spawns, cross-stream justifies exfils. Rings
survive only as a WFC prior for the D-gradient. **This is the grammar
the endgame map (doc 2) is written in.**

## Area 3 — Fauna/Ecology Lock (S16 finalization)

### Option 3A — Minimal ambient (ship-now shape)
Two species, one consequential side-effect each (rideable grazer wake;
bloom marks rich salvage), retaliate-only, no food web.
**Pros:** cheap; ships inside v0.3.1; side-effects are the highest
value-per-line (Stellaris lesson); zero dependence on locomotion proof.
**Cons:** world reads *inhabited*, not *alive*; no stories between
creatures.

### Option 3B — Food-web lite
3A plus: one predator species, nurseries as spawn homes (spawn-radius
handler), migration events on the Conductor, scavengers eat carcasses.
**Pros:** ecology stories (Herd & Predator windows, Scav Funerals) start
happening without the player; encounter entries #4/#14/#19/#29 become
real; still weight-table simple.
**Cons:** movement-dependent (execution caveat — post-Wave-1);
tuning surface doubles.

### Option 3C — Ecology-as-economy
Fauna are harvestable, herds are mobile cover, blooms are the treasure
map, nursery destruction has economy consequences.
**Pros:** fauna matter mechanically forever.
**Cons:** walks straight into Stellaris's trap — fauna as loot piñata;
harvesting invites gun-shaped verbs we don't have (no-guns caveat).

**Recommendation: 3A for v0.3.1, 3B as the v0.3.2 target, 3C never as a
system** — its good parts (blooms as treasure map, herds as cover) ship
as *encounter entries*, not as an economy.

## Area 4 — Expression Cluster (S21–S23 as one design)

### Option 4A — Registry-first
Build the telegraph registry as data; retrofit every existing effect
into it; audio/VFX/HUD become registry renderers.
**Pros:** systemic, permanent, enforceable in review; the census and
two-languages rules fall out automatically.
**Cons:** slow first visible win; risks becoming plumbing-before-taste.

### Option 4B — Set-piece vertical slice (RECOMMENDED first)
Fully express ONE encounter — the Wrecked Station — as a vertical slice:
its look (fabric-first silhouette, matte/rim discipline), its sound
(approach register, interior hush, unlock klaxon), its feedback (every
interaction telegraphed). Extract the registry FROM what survives taste.
**Pros:** taste-first and Greg-gateable in one screenshot/capture; feeds
the map + trailer work directly; the registry gets founded on proven
grammar instead of speculation.
**Cons:** one-off risk if the slice isn't productized immediately after.

### Option 4C — Channel-first
Build the three world-truth channels (event bus → audio register, fabric
tells, HUD census) and wire existing events through them.
**Pros:** matches the pressure-channel architecture already ratified;
mid-sized first win.
**Cons:** channels without content grammar invite inconsistent use.

**Recommendation: 4B → 4A.** Slice the station, extract the registry,
then productize. 4C happens implicitly as the slice's infrastructure.

## Area 5 — Chronicle + Knowledge Economy (S19 deepening)

### Option 5A — Ledger chronicle
Auto run records: cast, field, named death, cargo story. Zero verbs.
**Pros:** cheap substrate; already half-shipped (fragment pool).
**Cons:** a museum, not a game system.

### Option 5B — Knowledge-as-loot (RECOMMENDED core)
Echo buoys, ship logs, and survey fragments collected in-run enrich
future *briefings* for that map tier: the table you see sharpens (which
one-of-Z classes exist, exfil pool locations, key/lock pairings — never
live positions). "Learn the table" becomes literal collectible
progression, and it's pillar-safe: knowledge buys legibility, never
capability.
**Pros:** progression that survives wipes (the Marathon lesson: what
persists is reputation and knowledge); gives the hint layer (#10) a
meta-purpose; unbounded density (Outer Wilds lesson) without new space.
**Cons:** briefing UI grows; must not become mandatory homework —
knowledge accelerates, never gates.

### Option 5C — Echoes (the marquee)
Prior runs leave physical traces in future universes: your last wreck
drifts where you died (loot-bearing), your extraction beam scorched a
buoy, named rival kills are memorialized in chronicle fragments found by
others.
**Pros:** Run-It-Twice as poetry; the dying-universe theme gains memory;
community stories.
**Cons:** collides with the uniqueness digest (traces must not anchor
map layout); MP ownership questions; needs persistence infrastructure —
this is the equipment-layer era's feature.

**Recommendation:** 5A is the substrate (already underway), **5B is the
system to design next**, 5C is the marquee that reopens with
persistence. Sequence them; don't merge them.

## How tonight's map uses areas 1+2

The endgame map is authored under **1D + 2B/2C**: an off-map Singularity
gives it a downhill; its local drain is the drawn dominant; the map is a
constellation of set pieces laid on a watershed, spawns in slack water,
exfils cross-stream, the final exfil upstream — escape is a climb
against the fall. Full spec in `v0.3.1-endgame-map-undertow.md`.
