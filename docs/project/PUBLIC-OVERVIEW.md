# Last Singularity Public Overview

> A pitch and public-copy source for the website, store page, social bios, press blurbs, and devlog posts.

## Positioning

**Last Singularity** is a browser/WebGL extraction roguelike about piloting through the last surviving pockets of a collapsing universe. You do not fly through empty space. You fly through spacetime as a hostile, visible ocean: currents pull, gravity wells churn, stars become route anchors, and every confident burn of your engines spends the delta-v you may need to get home.

The core fantasy is simple: read the fabric, steal from the wreckage of dead civilizations, and escape before the universe notices you.

This is not a power fantasy about conquering space. It is a movement game about surviving it with style. The best pilot is not the one who holds thrust the longest; it is the one who knows when to drift, when to sling around a star, when to stay quiet, and when to abandon a rich wreck because the exits are starting to close.

## What Players Do

Each run drops the player into a doomed pocket universe with a rolled cosmic signature: maybe a slow, deceptive tide; maybe a shattered field already collapsing; maybe a vast deep where distance itself becomes the enemy. The map is filled with wrecks, gravity wells, stars, portals, AI scavengers, fauna, and a growing sense that the universe is running out of room.

The player searches for wrecks, extracts salvage, manages signal, spends or conserves delta-v, and chooses when to leave through unstable portals. Cargo becomes Exotic Matter and persistent progression if the player escapes. Death leaves the universe with everything they were carrying.

Moment to moment, the game is about movement decisions:

- Burn delta-v to start a route, then coast to preserve fuel.
- Catch currents in the ASCII fabric instead of fighting them.
- Snap into slingshot anchors and release at the right angle.
- Brake with reverse thrust only when the cost is worth it.
- Loot at speed, or slow down and risk getting caught.
- Stay quiet when the dark forest is listening.
- Leave early with a modest haul, or push one wreck too far.

## Store Feature Bullets

- **Surf a dying universe.** Spacetime behaves like a readable fluid field, not a static backdrop. Currents, wakes, gravity wells, and collapse events shape every route.
- **Movement is the game.** Thrust, braking, drift, flow coupling, and slingshots all compete inside a finite delta-v economy.
- **ASCII is the art direction.** The world is rendered through a WebGL fluid simulation and ASCII-dithered visual stack: terminal horror, scientific instrument, and cosmic ocean in one surface.
- **Chain slingshots across the map.** Stars, planetoids, comets, and black holes become route anchors. Engage, swing, release, and chain momentum through the terrain.
- **Extract before collapse.** Portals evaporate, wells grow, waves spread, viscosity rises, and the playable universe becomes less forgiving as the run continues.
- **Risk cargo, not just health.** Salvage only matters if it gets home. Death means lost cargo and lost gear, while extraction feeds the long-term hull and rig progression loop.
- **Five hull identities.** Drifter rides currents, Breacher brute-forces routes, Resonant manipulates the field, Shroud survives by staying unseen, and Hauler turns every successful escape into a vault-filling payday.
- **Signal has consequences.** Loud actions attract attention. Signal is not a mana bar or upgrade currency; it is the trail you leave for things hunting in the dark.
- **AI rivals share the run.** Scavengers and rival pilots compete for wrecks, react to the same hazards, and can turn a quiet route into a contested extraction.
- **Cosmic signatures make each run legible.** Every universe has a named personality with mechanical consequences: dense graveyards, fast collapses, thick spacetime, vast sparse deeps.
- **Persistent pilot progression.** Exotic Matter funds hull rig upgrades, while salvage and consumables create risky run-to-run loadout choices.
- **Built for fast iteration and playtesting.** The current stack is vanilla JavaScript, ES modules, browser/WebGL, and a server-authoritative simulation path for local and future networked play.

**Extraction Under Cosmic Pressure**  
Every run asks the same question differently: how much is enough? A safe exit preserves cargo. One more wreck might fund your next rig upgrade. It might also make you visible.

The five hulls are not just stat sheets. Each one changes how the player relates to the same physics: ride it, pierce it, bend it, hide from it, or haul value through it.

**A Roguelike About Leaving**  
The best decision is often not the biggest score. It is knowing when the route home is still real.

## Honest Public Boundaries

Use these guardrails until the build catches up to the whole long-term vision:

- Say **in development**.
- Sell solo extraction, AI rivals, movement mastery, and the ASCII fluid look first.
- Do not promise internet multiplayer yet. Local/private authority is real and slingshot authority is shipped, but public hosted sessions, matchmaking, and internet operations are still future work.
- Do not sell final balance, final maps, or a release date until the playtest pass says the movement economy is tuned.
- Prefer "browser/WebGL" over platform promises until the public website/download plan is chosen.
