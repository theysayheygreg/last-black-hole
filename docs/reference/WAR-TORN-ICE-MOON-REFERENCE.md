# War-Torn Ice Moon — Vibes & Themes Reference

> **Source:** *War-Torn Ice Moon* (Strange Dooms Backer Playtest V1, 2026-04-20). A 119-page expansion module for the FIST RPG. Author posture is comedic-grim cosmic horror in low-G ice-moon Europa setting.
>
> **Why it's here:** Greg flagged the *vibes and themes* (not the rules) as relevant to LBH. This is a mining log, not a cribbing log — pull what resonates with LBH's own gravitational / multiplayer / "Last Black Hole" frame; ignore the FIST-specific mechanics.

---

## The single biggest steal

> *"...warp cores let us reach ahead and borrow some timeless space from after the Black Hole Era; of course, doing so brings the BHE a little closer, but only a little."*

The **Black Hole Era** is named in WTIM as the implicit civilizational endpoint. Warp tech is *borrowing forward* — every use accelerates the universe toward its terminal state. **Greg's "Last Black Hole" name now has a sibling concept the player can feel:** every gravity-well manipulation in LBH could be implicitly understood as drawing the system closer to its final collapse. Players don't need to be told this directly; the world acts like it's true.

Carry-over for LBH:
- Make using gravity feel *consequential*, not just kinetic. The well you bend now is one less well that exists later.
- The "last" in *Last Black Hole* doesn't have to be lore-explained. It can be felt as *the thing you should not poke too hard, but you have to anyway.*
- A warp core in WTIM is *"a little wound in spacetime."* That's the right register for LBH's wells: wounds, not toys.

## The world's posture (worth importing, mostly intact)

WTIM's tonal compass is unusually compatible with LBH's existing house style (LBH already pulls from Returnal, EVE Wormhole, Stellaris). The full picture:

- **Heat as currency.** On Europa, you fight over heat the way other settings fight over food or fuel. *"Heat is one of the most fought-over resources on Europa (when the fighting has a reason at all)."* Every facility's heat source is a tactical fact. This generalizes cleanly to LBH: in deep space, the things that keep your body alive are the things you fight to control.
- **Glowing nights.** Europa's ice releases stored radiation at night and "shines an iridescent cool green." That's an aesthetic LBH can steal directly — quiet glow, not warning lights, as the nighttime palette in deep space.
- **Comedy as a survival posture.** The book is constantly funny without breaking tone. *"Sweets is convinced that this mess is the cosmic equivalent of a punchline."* / *"It should be a piece of cake for you guys."* / *"Ice War, Shmice War."* Despair-as-deadpan is a strong register for a roguelike-extraction game where every run probably ends badly.
- **Improvisation as the gameplay verb.** The referee's brief: *"FIST is firing on all cylinders when you roll with the punches by rolling on some tables mid-mission to see who just kicked down the door and why."* For LBH multiplayer this is a north star — design *for* mid-run improvisation, not against it.
- **Stories of escalating scale amid constant struggle.** Each session, players gain more enemies, allies, places, things, and ways to engage. *"Each session, your players will gain more..."* That's a procedural curve LBH already wants — every survived run leaves you with more hooks into the world, not fewer.

## Energies — the anomaly catalog

The most lift-able mechanical structure in WTIM, even though Greg said vibes-not-rules. The *shape* of it is the design pattern worth stealing:

Six **Energies** color Europa: COLD, METAGENESIS, JOVELIGHT, UNDERTOW, WARP RESIDUE, XENOPLASM. Each has:

| Layer | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Sources** | What gives you a level of this Energy (entering a contaminated zone, getting hit by an Energy weapon, drinking the wrong fluid). |
| **Remedies** | What removes a level (specific medical interventions, environmental antidotes, "anomalous means (risky)"). |
| **Symptoms** | What happens to you at each accumulated level. Levels 1-3 are quirks and disadvantages; 4-5 are major; 6 typically *ends your character's story*. |
| **Surge** | Risk roll: when something irritates the Energy, roll d6 vs. your level — if equal-or-under, the Surge effect happens (often loss-of-control or a personality lurch). |
| **Channel** | Voluntary risky use. Roll d6 vs. level — manifest a powerful effect, but failure means you lose control or worsen your condition. |
| **Energized Traits** | At high levels (or after triggering events), the Energy crystallizes into a permanent superpower-shaped corruption. |

**Why this matters for LBH:** the Energies catalog is a clean way to spec **environmental / cosmic-horror exposure as a stat-bearing system** without it becoming a generic "corruption meter." Each Energy has its own *flavor of damnation*, its own offer of power, and its own escalation arc. LBH could adopt this same shape for whatever cosmic effects gravity wells / black-hole proximity / multiplayer-ghost-traffic exposes the player to. Not "you're at 47% corrupted" — instead "you've got 3 GRAVITY-DRUNK and 2 ECHO-WORM, and the room is starting to ask you for things."

The six WTIM Energies, briefly, as direct vibe palette:

- **COLD** — preternatural ice cold that defies physics. Symptoms: numbness → stiffness → lung-pain → cognitive slowdown → collapse → quiet acceptance ("you collapse again, but decide it's okay now"). The ENERGIZED TRAITS turn you into ice itself (Iceman, Cryokinetic, Sculptor that carves a CRYOLITH ally from a block of ice). LBH analog: deep cold of intermellar drift.
- **METAGENESIS** — productivity-boost brainwaves gone wrong; turn workers irritable, then violent, then murderous. *"It was expected to excite the brain's neuroreceptors to associate hard work with feeling good."* The corporate horror is that this was *intentional*. LBH analog: any system that boosts player capability while corroding their judgment.
- **JOVELIGHT** — Jupiter's distorted radiation creates "martyric tendencies." Successive levels: confidence → boasting → urge to protect the weak → reduced self-preservation → primal need for a worthy foe → soul aches for a good death. **The whole arc is "becoming a hero in the worst way."** Beautiful tonal palette. LBH analog: any heroic-impulse loop that leads players to over-commit and get killed.
- **UNDERTOW** — psionic emanations from "something asleep in the Undersea." Symptoms ladder: bright lights hurt → vertigo → memory lapses → fear of vacuum → constant amnesia → terminal burrowing into a bottomless pit. **The horror is that the world is dreaming you.** Channel: replace any d6 roll with your UNDERTOW score. LBH analog: cosmic-scale horror where the player is incidental to something vast and asleep.
- **WARP RESIDUE** — ripples in spacetime from the Warp Station's destruction. Effects: deja-vu → misplacing objects → spatial reasoning weakens → electric sparks from fingertips → can't see your own reflection → *"you remember the loop — it's happening again!"* SURGE table includes "switch places with nearest person," "evil clone of you with 1 HP blips into reality," and *"Something's wrong; the next time any player rolls a 6, they roll on this table."* This is the closest sibling to LBH's gravity-and-time vibe and worth lifting most directly.
- **XENOPLASM** — biology-altering psionic fluid left by an extinct alien species (the squidmen). Levels: faint whispers → migraines when alone → unusually bendy joints → soft bones → green thick blood → *"the host is ready, viscerally explode into a new squidman."* Channel: heal someone you're touching. The player's body becomes a vector for a dead civilization's reproduction. **Beautiful and unsettling.** LBH analog: any "the cosmos uses you" mechanic.

Don't import all six. Pick one or two whose flavor maps to LBH's actual mood — probably WARP RESIDUE and UNDERTOW, possibly XENOPLASM if multiplayer wants a "the dead are reaching through you" register.

## The locations as image bank

WTIM's setpiece "Conflict Zones" are mostly inert as mechanics but loaded as imagery. The ones LBH could draw from:

- **The Subjovian Point.** A spot on Europa where Jupiter hangs directly overhead. Pilgrims, devotees, glory-seekers fight perpetually for the right to "stand face-to-face with Jove." The Hill at the center is built from packed bodies and snow. *"Perijove wants to see the combined effects of Jovelight and Metagenesis. Their corporate HQ ... has a view of the Subjovian Point every 10 hours or so. When victors are atop the mound, roll 1D6; on a 1, one among them is shot with Metagenic Waves from Perijove HQ."* — corporate sniper as god. **Image bank for LBH:** any hero moment in multiplayer that draws unwanted observation from a higher entity.
- **The Warp Station Husk.** Titanic ship hull torn open in orbit, raining innards on Europa forever. Scavengers, pilots, blockade ships. **Image bank for LBH:** a wreck so big it functions as terrain.
- **Norhaven Plaza.** A planned community that wasn't finished by Warp Day. Its name was chosen to appeal to offworld investors — *"there's no ocean to view."* Half-built skyscrapers, scavengers in alleys, panicked shots. **Image bank for LBH:** abandoned-by-capitalism aesthetic; the dream of a city that was never inhabited.
- **The Termitarium.** A maze of tunnels under the ice, *"theoretically convenient... but its fame has made it crowded with hundreds of warbands and squads attempting the same thing. It's a distillation of the paranoid hostility found on the surface, heightened by blindness, hungry predators, and the distant echoes of lost battalions."* **Image bank for LBH:** any player route shortcut that's also where you most likely die.
- **Yeti Coliseum.** Honor combat by ancient yeti law. Winners drink from amphoras of "Ancient Fermented Yeti Blood" to heal; losers are spared if rules were followed. **Image bank for LBH:** any ritual-combat zone where rules are enforced by social pressure rather than mechanics.
- **Steeplechase Nexus Entertainment System.** A single working horse-racing arcade in an empty ice field. *"There are exceedingly few lasting truces on Europa, and almost no known location with unquestioned, untouchable peace. The Steeplechase Nexus is one such holy place: there is an unspoken, unbroken truce within a mile of it."* **The most powerful single location in the whole book.** A pre-war machine, still working, around which everyone tacitly agrees not to kill. **Image bank for LBH:** sanctuary by accident; rare safe spaces preserved by collective superstition.
- **The Cradle.** A dreamlike grey-mist forest of ice columns and snow elves that comes to find players "when they are ready." Following the snow elf ends your character's story peacefully — no death, just *gone*. Refusing leaves a snow elf egg at your feet. **The ultimate offering of escape from suffering.** This is the heaviest single image in the book and a perfect LBH companion: a place a player could *choose* to leave forever, with no fanfare, no death animation, just the world quietly accepting them.

## The Undersea (and the thing asleep in it)

Worth its own pull. The Undersea is the body of liquid water under Europa's ice crust. WTIM's framing:

> *"The answer must lie in the Undersea, where top scientists and mystics hypothesize that some thing — not something — under the Ice is deep in slumber. Before Warp Day, Sublime was making a tidy profit using organic chemical compounds found in the Undersea in a seasonal carbo flavor without knowing the source; maybe a higher-up there knows more. If the Undertow is truly the dreamspace emanations of some godlike being, we must pray that the war does not wake it."*

Three things here are good to lift:

1. **"Some thing — not something."** That awkward grammar is exactly the right shape for cosmic horror. The thing is *not a thing* — it is *that thing*. LBH's central black hole could carry this same construction: not "the black hole" but "the thing that the black hole used to be."
2. **Industry was using its emanations for soda flavoring.** Capitalism unwittingly bottling the dreams of a god. That's the kind of detail that makes the world feel lived-in and contemptibly funny. LBH multiplayer could have small-business equivalents — players selling ground-up cosmic dread as commodity.
3. **"Pray that the war does not wake it."** The biggest threat in the world is the thing the world doesn't know is a threat yet. LBH's late-game can have this shape: the thing you've been gravitating around isn't dead, isn't asleep — it's *not paying attention yet.*

## Tone-bank quotes worth keeping

Things to lift verbatim or as direct inspiration for LBH copy:

- **On Europa as setting:** *"Europa is an infinite conflict zone where everyone is trying to get one over on everyone."*
- **On the player's situation:** *"Boxed in from orbit and hunted on the surface, you do what FIST does best: improvise."*
- **On scale:** *"Each session, your players will gain more enemies, allies, places to go, things to snag, and ways to engage with their environment."*
- **On survival as flavor:** *"It's not necessarily a hardcore survival sim... but once you have the tools to walk or drive around, someone else will notice and get mad."*
- **On the war's shape:** *"Some days you just want to get from A to B but there's a war in the way."*
- **On the Cradle's offer:** *"If you choose to follow the snow elf, you do not return... Your story, and your suffering, are over. If you do not approach, the snow elf will droop sadly and shuffle back behind the column. The Cradle will dissipate, and at your feet is a perfectly spherical snow elf egg."*
- **On the Black Hole Era:** *"...we get a little wound in spacetime called a warp core. It lets us reach ahead and borrow some timeless space from after the Black Hole Era; of course, doing so brings the BHE a little closer, but only a little."*

## Things to NOT lift

A few WTIM elements that are setting-specific and would clash with LBH if imported directly:

- **Squidmen as a cosmic horror.** WTIM's extinct-alien-species framing is great in its own context; LBH's cosmic horror probably wants to live closer to gravity / time / black-hole physics rather than xeno-biology.
- **The corporate name comedy** (Sublime, Tephrax, Halcyon Biologics, Accent Solutions, etc.). Funny in WTIM, would feel borrowed if dropped into LBH. LBH can have its own corporate satire; it shouldn't crib WTIM's specifically.
- **The yeti coliseum's specific honor code.** Beautiful in WTIM, but porting it would feel like cosplay. The *image* of accidental sanctuary is worth lifting; the specific honor mechanic isn't.
- **The Premade Characters and their interview transcripts.** These are too FIST-shaped (ULTRA traits, WAR DICE, role tags) to map cleanly. The *idea* of capsule character intros via transcript fragments is cute and worth borrowing as a presentation layer; the specific cast isn't.

## What to actually do with this in LBH

If/when LBH wants to lean into the cosmic-horror register more, this reference is the closest thing in the existing reference shelf to a "world that punishes the player without explaining itself." Specific carry-overs worth piloting:

1. **Adopt the Black Hole Era as implicit endgame language.** Every meaningful gravity manipulation accelerates *something* — the player can feel it without being told what.
2. **Pick 1-2 Energies to model.** WARP RESIDUE and UNDERTOW are the two most LBH-compatible. Build them as catalog-style anomaly classes per the SOURCES / REMEDIES / SYMPTOMS / SURGE / CHANNEL pattern.
3. **Plant a "Cradle"-class location.** A place a player can choose to leave the run forever, peacefully, with no death animation. Asymmetric to multiplayer (the team continues; you don't return). One of the most powerful single design moves in the WTIM book.
4. **Use the Undersea framing for the central black hole.** *"Some thing — not something."* It is sleeping. The war/combat doesn't matter to it yet. Late-game = it starts paying attention.
5. **Borrow the improvisation posture.** Design FOR mid-run table-rolls and emergent absurdity, not against them. *"There's no wrong way to play, but [this game] is firing on all cylinders when you roll with the punches."*
